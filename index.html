Executive Summary

Experimental investigation of the aerodynamic characteristics of a Clark Y-14 airfoil in a low-speed, incompressible wind tunnel environment. The primary objective was to determine lift, drag, and pitching moment characteristics across a range of freestream velocities and angles of attack, including pre-stall and post-stall behavior.

Objectives & Problem Framing

  • Evaluate aerodynamic performance under subsonic conditions while maintaining precise control over freestream properties.
  • Compare independent aerodynamic measurement techniques for cross-validation.
  • Perform rigorous uncertainty analysis and wind tunnel calibration.

Methods & Tools

  • Wind Tunnel Calibration: Established the relationship between percent fan power input and freestream velocity using Pitot-static measurements and Bernoulli's equation.
  • Pressure Integration: Obtained surface pressure distributions through pressure taps distributed along the chord, allowing aerodynamic force and moment coefficients to be computed through numerical integration.
  • Sting Balance: Directly measured axial force, normal force, and pitching moment using a strain-gage sting balance.
  • Wake Survey: Employed the wake-survey method to estimate drag through downstream momentum deficit analysis.

Key Takeaways & Conclusions

  • Method Consistency: Comparison between pressure-integration, wake-survey, and direct sting-balance measurements provided internal validation of the experimental methods.
  • Aerodynamic Behavior: Demonstrated the linear increase of lift coefficient with angle of attack in pre-stall conditions, which is consistent with thin airfoil theory. Increasing the airspeed over the airfoils delayed the angle of attack at which maximum lift occurred and also delayed stall conditions.
  • Incompressible Flow Validation: Confirmed that the pressure coefficient distribution is largely independent of freestream velocity when properly nondimensionalized, which is consistent with incompressible flow theory.
