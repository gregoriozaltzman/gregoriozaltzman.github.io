Executive Summary

Conceptual design study for an ultra-efficient blended-wing-body (BWB) commercial transport named "Manta", focused on configuration trades, propulsion-airframe integration, and performance-driven sizing. The design targets a 7,400 nmi long-haul route carrying 250 passengers at Mach 0.85, significantly improving upon traditional Tube and Wing (TAW) architectures.

Design Optimization & Analysis

  • Gradient-Free Optimization: Utilized Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) via Python to maximize cruise L/D, improving the baseline from 24.85 to an optimized 25.30 while satisfying strict material yield stress and stability constraints.
  • Sensitivity Analysis: Conducted a Monte Carlo simulation using Latin Hypercube sampling to map the design space, revealing that aspect ratio has the greatest influence on L/D, while chord length drives cost per seat-mile.
  • Stability & Control: Implemented a digital Fly-By-Wire (FBW) active control system to counteract the inherent dynamic instabilities of the reflexed center body and outer wing sections.

Key Engineering Decisions

  • Cabin Architecture: A triple-aisle (2-4-4-2) main cabin configuration housed within a PRSEUS (Pultruded Rod Stitched Efficient Unitized Structures) reinforced fuselage to handle asymmetric internal pressurization.
  • Propulsion Integration: Twin GEnX-1B high-bypass turbofan engines mounted on the aft upper-surface to maximize boundary layer ingestion (BLI) and provide acoustic shielding.
  • Materials: Carbon Fiber Reinforced Polymer (CFRP) and High Modulus Carbon Fiber used for primary lifting structures to minimize empty weight.

Executive Summary

Detailed design of a profit-maximizing RC aircraft, "Citadel", featuring a conventional tube-and-wing architecture. The design prioritizes volume payload capacity with a secondary emphasis on minimizing flight time and maximizing weight payload.

Design Optimization & Analysis

  • Multidisciplinary Design Optimization (MDO): Employed a gradient-based solver in Python (JAX, modOpt, IPOPT) to evaluate 24 constraints across aerodynamics, stability, electrical, and physical packaging to maximize profit ($/hr).
  • Sensitivity Analysis: Sampled the design space 1e9 times via Monte Carlo simulation, using a Spearman Rank Correlation to prove volume payload and flight time as the most influential parameters.
  • Aerodynamics: Utilized a high-lift S1223 airfoil optimized via Neural Foil to maximize lift and aerodynamic efficiency, extrapolated to 3D using Prandtl's lifting line correction.

Key Engineering Decisions

  • Payload Deployment: Implemented a twin-bay door deployment system powered by dual servos to release a large 6.25 L volume payload mid-flight.
  • Structures: Built-up wooden fuselage with 14 equidistant ribs, supported by carbon-fiber rods and spruce spars to prevent buckling, coupled with an XPS foam wing.
  • Propulsion: Integrated a SunnySky X2216 V3 brushless motor with a 10x7E APC propeller, enclosed by a 3D-printed PLA nosecone for drag reduction.

Executive Summary

Conducted Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) simulations to investigate the thermal dissipation effectiveness of the Project Maelstrom vortex-cooled rocket engine. This cooling system utilizes oxidizer injection to generate a protective swirling flow field between the chamber wall and the hotter core flow.

Key Technical Contributions

  • Heat Transfer Analysis: Ran analyses across three flow conditions (maximum, half, and quarter) to evaluate the influence of oxidizer mass flow rates on vortex formation.
  • Thermal Mapping: Mapped chamber, cross, nozzle, and plenum plane temperatures to quantify insulation effectiveness.
  • Flow Thresholds: Demonstrated that a minimum threshold of oxidizer flow is required to sustain the vortex structure; lower flow rates caused the vortex to decay, exposing the chamber walls to core combustion temperatures.

Executive Summary

Experimental investigation of the aerodynamic characteristics of a Clark Y-14 airfoil in a low-speed, incompressible wind tunnel environment. The primary objective was to determine lift, drag, and pitching moment characteristics across a range of freestream velocities and angles of attack, including pre-stall and post-stall behavior.

Objectives & Problem Framing

  • Evaluate aerodynamic performance under subsonic conditions while maintaining precise control over freestream properties.
  • Compare independent aerodynamic measurement techniques for cross-validation.
  • Perform rigorous uncertainty analysis and wind tunnel calibration.

Methods & Tools

  • Wind Tunnel Calibration: Established the relationship between percent fan power input and freestream velocity using Pitot-static measurements and Bernoulli's equation.
  • Pressure Integration: Obtained surface pressure distributions through pressure taps distributed along the chord, allowing aerodynamic force and moment coefficients to be computed through numerical integration.
  • Sting Balance: Directly measured axial force, normal force, and pitching moment using a strain-gage sting balance.
  • Wake Survey: Employed the wake-survey method to estimate drag through downstream momentum deficit analysis.
